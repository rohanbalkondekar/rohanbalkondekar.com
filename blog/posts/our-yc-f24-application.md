### **Idea**

**Q. Why did you pick this idea to work on? Do you have domain expertise in this area? How do you know people need what you’re making?**

— Each of us in a developer’s capacity has dealt with legacy code and it’s always frustrating to see someone else’s code written 10–15 years ago without any documentation.

I remember at Nuance (now Microsoft), we had this n-gram-based language model written in Perl, a relic from 15 years ago that was still running in production, and every time we had a bug there we literally had to spend the whole day looking into it.

This kind of problem costs both developers and enterprises, in fact software development teams spend 17.3 hrs/week and 50% of the development budget.

We believe we occupy a unique position at the intersection of the problem space (Legacy code) & Solution space (AI). We deal with legacy code pains and fix them everyday in our professional lives, and we have been developing AI solutions for a range of use-cases.

For Example: We have integrated features into EPIC systems and powerscribe (Radiology product with 80% market share in US) dealing with all the legacy code these system have been developed on and we also have members in our team that were core developer of the nursing AI copilot for EPIC systems and fine tuned small models for those reasons.

Another team member of ours is an AI product manager who has previously worked at a consulting firm and has managed multiple legacy code modernization projects.

We have received a lot of organic inbound and interest from enterprises and individuals alike for the product that we are developing and we already have our first paying customer.

**Q. Who are your competitors? What do you understand about your business that they don’t?**

**—** Our main competitors include code generation platforms like Devin, GitHub Copilot, Codium, poolside.ai and magic.dev.

These tools primarily focus on generating new code or refactoring small parts of existing code. However, they often overlook the complexities involved in modernizing and integrating legacy code, which is where we set ourselves apart.

While tools like Second.dev have started addressing the challenge of code migration, they are not focusing on the larger issue of legacy languages like C, C++, and COBOL — languages that are deeply rooted in industries like healthcare, banking, and telecom.

These legacy systems present significant security vulnerabilities and are a trillion-dollar problem that requires more than just basic code translation.
